**Faster**
- KosmicKrisp keeps the pipelines Metal builds for a game in archives of the game's own, next to its shader cache, instead of relying on Metal's cache, which all games share and which holds only about 2.4 GB. After another game ran, Rise of the Tomb Raider no longer rebuilds its 13,659 pipelines during play: its benchmark takes 125 instead of 227 s. The first start of a game after the update fills its archives; Rise of the Tomb Raider's take about 850 MB.
- With complete archives, the shaders load as compiled AIR too instead of being compiled again: Rise of the Tomb Raider runs at 27.7–28.1 instead of 26.6 fps on average, Geothermal Valley at 23.7–24.4 instead of 21.7 fps, with fewer stutters (1 % low 17–18 instead of 14 fps).
- Robust constant buffer reads no longer branch: the most expensive render pass of Horizon Zero Dawn takes 2 instead of 15 ms on the GPU (the game's frame rate is limited elsewhere for now).

**Fixed**
- Metal leaves a working copy of every pipeline capture in Wine's cache and never removes it; KosmicKrisp now removes them (`MESA_KK_KEEP_STALE_COPIES=1` keeps them).

**For developers**
- Mesa patches 0038–0040; measurements, Horizon Zero Dawn's GPU profile and memory budget, and notes on benchmarking a fanless Mac are in `docs/MACOS_PORT.md`. `MESA_KK_PIPELINE_ARCHIVES=0` turns the archives off, `MESA_KK_COMPILE_STATS=1` reports pipelines from archives and shaders from AIR.
- Wine's D3D9 tests and the D3D11 tests give the same results as with v0.1.0.15; vkd3d-proton's test suite fails no other tests.
